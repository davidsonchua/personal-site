Google Knowledge Panel playbook

A prioritised, six-month plan to make davidsonchua.cc rank #1 for “Davidson Chua” and to give Google enough confidence to generate a person Knowledge Panel. Ordered by impact — do them in this order.

Read this first — the honest version. There is no application form for a Knowledge Panel. Google generates them automatically when its Knowledge Graph is confident that a distinct entity exists and that the web agrees on its core facts. Adding Person schema does not create one — that's the single most common piece of SEO folklore. What creates one is consistency at volume: one authoritative page about you (your new /about), and many independent sources repeating the same name, title, company, education and location, all pointing back at it.

Realistic timeline for a founder with no Wikipedia article: 9–24 months, with a real chance it never triggers. Ranking #1 for your own name is far more achievable — expect that within weeks of deploying, and it's most of the practical benefit anyway.

Phase 1

Deploy & get indexed

Week 1 · ~90 minutes

Nothing else matters until Google has crawled the new pages and settled on one canonical hostname.

  1. Fix the www / apex split Critical

    Right now both davidsonchua.cc and www.davidsonchua.cc serve the identical page with no redirect between them. That is textbook duplicate content — it splits your ranking signals across two hostnames, and it's why Google indexed the www version while your own sitemap pointed at the apex.

    I've made the apex (davidsonchua.cc) canonical everywhere — it matches what your sitemap and robots.txt already used, and it's the shorter thing to say out loud. In the Vercel dashboard → Project → Settings → Domains, set www.davidsonchua.cc to Redirect to davidsonchua.cc (308).

    Prefer www instead? It's one line — change BASE in _tools/build.py and re-run the build.

  2. Fix the broken /about route Critical

    davidsonchua.cc/about currently returns a 404. Your vercel.json rewrites /about → /about.html, but about.html was deleted in a past commit and the rewrite was never removed. Google has been hitting a dead URL that your own sitemap advertises.

    The new vercel.json I've included drops that stale rewrite. The About page is now a real file at about/index.html, which Vercel serves at /about under your existing cleanUrls setting.

  3. Check email obfuscation if you're behind Cloudflare

    You hit this before: Scrape Shield rewrites mailto: links into JavaScript, so Google sees a broken email on your contact page. If Cloudflare sits in front of Vercel, Dashboard → your domain → Scrape Shield → turn off Email Address Obfuscation. If you're on Vercel DNS only, nothing to do.

  4. Verify in Google Search Console Critical

    Use domain property verification via DNS TXT record, not the URL-prefix method — it covers both hostnames and every subdomain at once. This is also the single verification channel Google accepts when you later claim the Knowledge Panel, so it's a prerequisite twice over.

    • Submit https://davidsonchua.cc/sitemap.xml under Sitemaps
    • Use URL Inspection → Request indexing on / and /about
    • Do not use the sitemap ping URL — Google deprecated it in 2023
  5. Validate the structured data

    Paste each URL into the Rich Results Test and schema.org validator. I've already checked the JSON parses and that no @id reference dangles, but confirm against the live pages after deploy.

  6. Add Bing Webmaster Tools Worth 10 min

    Import directly from Search Console. Bing also powers ChatGPT search, so this is your cheapest route into AI answers. Submit the sitemap and enable IndexNow while you're there (Google doesn't support IndexNow — Bing, Yandex and several AI crawlers do).

Phase 3

Wikidata

Month 1 · ~2 hours · highest-leverage single action

Wikidata is the most machine-readable input into the Knowledge Graph, openly licensed and heavily reused. It is not a switch that turns a panel on, but practitioners consistently find it cuts the amount of other corroboration needed by roughly half.

  1. Understand what qualifies you

    You do not need a Wikipedia article. Wikidata notability has three routes and you only need one. Yours is criterion 2: a “clearly identifiable entity that can be described using serious and publicly available references.” Your NUS Computing feature, Crunchbase record, ACRA-registered company and press appearances are exactly that kind of reference.

    Verify the current policy at Wikidata:Notability before you start — there's an open RFC that could tighten criterion 2.

  2. Create the item correctly Critical

    Use a personal, non-branded account. Branded accounts and undisclosed promotional editing get items deleted, and a deleted item is worse than no item — it leaves a negative trail. Reference every single claim; unreferenced claims on a living person are the number-one deletion trigger.

    Minimum viable item:

    PropertyValue
    Label / DescriptionDavidson Chua / Singaporean entrepreneur, founder of Influencees
    instance of (P31)human (Q5)
    country of citizenship (P27)Singapore
    occupation (P106)entrepreneur, businessperson
    educated at (P69)National University of Singapore; Singapore Polytechnic
    official website (P856)https://davidsonchua.cc
    Crunchbase person ID (P2087)davidson-chua-cc
    LinkedIn personal profile ID (P6634)davidsonchua
    X username (P2002)davidsonchua (only if confirmed)

    External identifiers are the real currency here — each one is an independent authority record anchoring you as a unique entity.

  3. Create items for the companies too

    Create Influencees and Autosave Pte. Ltd. as items, with founded by (P112) pointing at your item and country (P17) Singapore. Relationship density is how Google separates you from other people with your name — an isolated node is easy to confuse, a connected subgraph isn't.

  4. Add different from (P1889)

    If any other Davidson Chua has a Wikidata item, link yours to theirs with P1889 and give both distinct descriptions. Explicit disambiguation is more effective than hoping volume sorts it out.

  5. Then add the Q-ID to your site

    Once the item is live and stable for a couple of weeks, send me the Q-ID. I'll add https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q… to your sameAs array and as an identifier. Schema.org names the Wikidata URI as the canonical example of an unambiguous identity reference — it's the strongest single entry in that list.

Phase 5

Claim it

When the panel appears
  1. Search your name in an incognito window, monthly

    Look for a panel on the right (desktop) or above results (mobile). Set a monthly reminder — I can schedule one if you'd like.

  2. Click “Claim this knowledge panel”

    Verify by signing in to an official property. Search Console is the channel you fully control, which is why Phase 1 set it up. YouTube, X and Facebook also work.

    You can't edit a panel directly even once verified — you suggest changes with supporting evidence and Google reviews them. Note that Google states not all panels are claimable.

  3. Expect volatility

    Panels disappear. A conflicting new source, a profile going offline, or a confidence dip can remove one. The fix is always the same: more consistent corroboration, not more markup.

Don't bother with these

  • FAQ schema. Dead — Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in May 2026 and removed the documentation. Plenty of 2026 SEO blogs still recommend it.
  • A Google Business Profile for yourself. That's a business listing system; it cannot produce a person entity. One for Autosave is fine on its own merits.
  • Writing your own Wikipedia article. Autobiography is a deletion magnet and the notability bar (significant coverage in multiple genuinely independent sources) is one you don't clear yet. Wikidata is the right door.
  • Chasing Core Web Vitals scores. Thresholds are unchanged (LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1) and your site now sails past them. Field data comes from real-user traffic, so a site your size likely has none to be judged on. Ignore the “Core Web Vitals 2026 changed!” posts — they're content farming.
  • The Google Indexing API. Restricted to job postings and broadcast events. Using it here does nothing.
  • Paying an agency for a “guaranteed” knowledge panel. Nobody can guarantee one. Paid Wikidata/Wikipedia editing and fabricated credentials get detected and set you backwards.
  • Sitemap priority and changefreq. Google ignores both.
